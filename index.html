From the Regular Season to the World Series: Which Fans Face the Bigger Affordability Shock?

Milwaukee and Tampa Bay have almost identical median household incomes, but the cost of attending their games has taken two very different paths. Here's how the price of four tickets compares across three stages of the season.

THE BIGGEST AFFORDABILITY JUMP

59.3×

On the published ticket benchmarks, the Brewers' four-ticket affordability burden rises 59.3-fold from the regular season to the World Series entry-price level.

The corresponding change for Rays fans is 15.1×. Milwaukee experiences the larger rise in both percentage points and multiples of its regular-season burden.

THE TAKEAWAY

Milwaukee's affordability burden grew more sharply

Brewers: 2.7% → 27.1% → 160.1% of median monthly household income.

Rays: 3.3% → 29.6% → 49.4% of median monthly household income.

Regular season to World Series: Milwaukee increased by 157.4 percentage points of monthly household income, versus 46.1 percentage points in Tampa Bay.

Postseason snapshot to World Series: Milwaukee rises from 27.1% to 160.1%; Tampa Bay rises from 29.6% to 49.4%.

By the World Series, Milwaukee's four-ticket burden is about 3.2 times Tampa Bay's on this get-in benchmark, even though annual median metro household incomes differ by just $356.

Milwaukee median household income: $77,919 annually ($6,493 per month).

Tampa Bay median household income: $78,275 annually ($6,523 per month).

METHODOLOGY & SOURCES

Our calculation: Each four-ticket estimate equals 4 × the relevant single-ticket benchmark. We divided this by annual metro median household income / 12 to obtain the share of a typical month's gross household income.

Three distinct price definitions: Regular season uses the team's regular-season average; playoffs use previously collected average postseason resale asking prices; World Series uses the mean of the three sources' reported get-in prices, averaged across each finalist's two guaranteed home games. These are different price statistics and observation windows; the stage-to-stage changes are indicative affordability benchmarks, not a controlled like-for-like price index. Earlier postseason prices are from the pre-playoff snapshot rather than actual completed-game transactions.

World Series sources: TicketNews / Ticket Club; WUSF / SeatData.io; RateYourSeats. Published asking-price snapshots from October 20–21, 2026. Sources may differ in fee inclusions. TicketNews, WUSF and RateYourSeats.

Other sources: U.S. Census Bureau's metro household income estimates, plus Action Network's 2026 MLB family cost study for regular-season outing context.

All figures are estimates based on published listings, not completed sales. World Series get-in prices may represent standing-room inventory. Travel, parking, accommodation and concessions are excluded from ticket-only estimates. Prices can change before each game.
